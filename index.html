Reaching Beyond Earth

Satellites transformed how humans communicate, navigate, study Earth, and explore space.

What Is a Satellite?

A satellite is an object that orbits another object in space. Natural satellites include moons, while artificial satellites are machines built by humans and placed into orbit. Artificial satellites collect information, transmit signals, and help scientists understand our planet and the universe.

The Beginning of a New Era

On October 4, 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the first artificial satellite to orbit Earth. This achievement marked a major turning point in space exploration and intensified the Space Race between the Soviet Union and the United States.

Sputnik demonstrated that humans could send technology into orbit, opening the door to future satellite communication, weather forecasting, and navigation.

Why Satellites Matter

Satellites help people predict weather, communicate across long distances, use GPS navigation, monitor natural disasters, and observe changes in Earth's environment. Many everyday technologies depend on satellites.
